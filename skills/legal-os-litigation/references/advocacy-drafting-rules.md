# Advocacy drafting rules

This is the advocacy-document supplement to the existing litigation workflow. It
does not replace R1–R16, the workspace contract, the case-research handoff, the
external-expression boundary, the template runtime, or the pleading quality
gate. Read it when `document_variant` is one of:

- `advocacy-brief` — a complete agency/advocacy submission;
- `pre-hearing-advocacy-opinion` — a pre-hearing advocacy opinion;
- `post-hearing-advocacy-brief` — a post-hearing advocacy brief/opinion; or
- `supplemental-advocacy-opinion` — a focused supplemental or special advocacy opinion.

The selector is a routing and drafting label, not a template approval. Continue
to classify `procedure_type`, `pleading_role`, `pleading_stage`, scope and
delivery intent under R1/R8. Formal-file generation still requires one exact
approved procedure-specific template; a missing advocacy template remains
`TEMPLATE_REQUIRED`.
That status holds formal-file generation only; source-grounded analysis and a
clearly labelled text working draft may proceed under R2 and the workspace
contract while template approval is unresolved.

## Core position and boundaries

The advocate's position is partisan, the reasoning path is adjudicative, the
expression is judicial-document style, and the conclusion serves the client:
**立场是代理人的，论证逻辑是法官的，表达方式是裁判文书的，论证结论是有利于委托人的。**
Objective reasoning does not turn the advocacy submission into a neutral report
or a judgment written in the adjudicator's voice.

Use the current supplied materials, verified law and confirmed litigation
position. A favourable interpretation may be selected where facts, evidence and
law reasonably support it, but the submission must not invent facts, distort
evidence, misapply law, evade mandatory disclosure, admit liability, waive a
right, concede a material fact, or introduce a material fallback without the
confirmation required by R11. Keep full adverse-risk analysis internally and
select only the lawful, accurate and necessary external reasoning under the
external-expression boundary.

## A01 — Independent document function and scope

An advocacy submission integrates the material facts, evidence, contract terms
where applicable, law and necessary adjudicative rules into reasons the
decision-maker can lawfully adopt. It is not a copy of a complaint, answer,
hearing record, evidence comment or internal case memo.

For a complete advocacy brief, cover every independent request and dispute in
the confirmed scope. A pre-hearing, post-hearing or supplemental opinion must
fully resolve the designated issues without mechanically repeating the whole
case. For a represented defendant/respondent whose answer has already been
filed, the default post-hearing brief is a two-source supplement of that answer:
take the answer as the baseline and cover material actual oral assertions and
late-filed written opinions within the confirmed scope. This default is not a
fresh whole-case rewrite. An explicit whole-case scope overrides the
answer-based default and an explicit narrower scope selects the focused
supplemental variant. Use R2's conclusions table and existing matter records to
determine the scope; do not create a new advocacy ledger.

## A02 — Client stance with adjudicative reasoning

Organize the argument for the represented party. In a genuine range of lawful
interpretations, use the supported interpretation that best advances the
confirmed objective. Use objective, restrained language and a decision-maker's
reasoning sequence, while retaining the advocate's voice and stance. The core
motto above is a stance-and-method constraint, not permission to write as the
court or to pre-judge the result.

Internal research must test adverse facts, evidence, authority, cases and
litigation risk. External text should disclose what is truthful, accurate,
lawful and necessary for the procedural function. Do not volunteer internal
strategy, settlement limits, contingency paths, an unraised opponent theory or
an unconfirmed admission merely because it might be relevant. This boundary
does not excuse a required procedural response or a truthful mandatory
disclosure; apply the external-expression boundary and R13–R16 where they
actually govern.

## A03 — Reverse-design a lawful adjudicative path

Before formal drafting, use the existing R3 chain to work backwards from the
confirmed objective:

`objective → required adjudicative conclusion → legal rule/elements → material facts → evidence basis → viable argument path`

This is a design of a lawful reasoning path, not a way to manufacture the
facts. If evidence or legal support is insufficient, identify the gap and its
effect. The external advocacy text proceeds forward from facts, evidence and
law and retains the R5 conclusion-led heading and paragraph discipline. Do not
create a second reverse-analysis workflow, form or ledger.

## A04 — Identify decision-critical variables

For each core dispute, identify the factual, legal, evidentiary or calculation
issue that could determine or materially change liability, scope, amount or
the procedural result. A counterfactual test is useful: if the decision-maker
made the opposite finding, could the result change?

Allocate research and drafting depth to those variables rather than treating
background facts and dispositive issues equally. Reuse `cn-case-hub` decision-
fork variables and the existing research handoff when available; otherwise
identify the variables directly from this matter's facts, evidence and legal
elements. Do not add a separate decision-variable table or make case research a
prerequisite to identifying the variables.

## A05 — Select compatible argument paths

When several supported reasons advance the same outcome, classify them for
working purposes as:

- decisive reasoning;
- independently valuable supporting reasoning;
- reinforcing reasoning; and
- low-value or high-risk reasoning.

Use the most persuasive compatible reason as the spine and retain additional
reasons that have independent adjudicative value. Test factual, evidentiary,
legal and positional compatibility; do not omit a useful reason merely to be
short or pile up decorative reasons merely to be complete. An independent
supporting reason is not automatically an alternative defence. A material
alternative, admission, waiver or concession remains subject to R11 and the
required user confirmation.

## A06 — Substantive legal subsumption and a closed conclusion

Inherit R3, R5 and the existing general argument model. Do not create a second
parallel six-step model or force six headings into the document. For every core
dispute, the reasoning must functionally do all of the following:

1. identify the concrete issue to be decided;
2. establish the applicable legal rule, contract term and adjudicative standard;
3. extract the material facts supported by evidence;
4. explain why those facts satisfy or fail the rule (legal subsumption);
5. answer a material opposing proposition that could change the result; and
6. state the advocacy conclusion and its effect on the request, defence,
   amount or procedural relief.

Do not list facts, evidence and provisions and then jump to a conclusion. Do
not stop at “consider comprehensively”, “further review is needed” or similar
review directions when the available material supports an evaluation. Those
phrases remain appropriate when a genuine evidence gap, required investigation
or unresolved legal condition exists; state the limitation precisely. Return
the conclusion to the concrete adjudicative objective.

## A07 — Evidence evaluation and burden reasoning

Inherit the existing fact states, evidence map, source verification, proof-scope
and conclusions-table controls. For material evidence, state what it proves,
what it cannot prove, and whether it is corroborated or contradicted by other
evidence. Keep authenticity, legality, relevance and probative force distinct:
accepting authenticity does not accept the opponent's purpose or weight.

When relying on failure to prove, state the fact's legal significance, the
burden holder, the applicable proof standard, what the current material fails
to establish and the resulting legal consequence. Failure to prove a fact is
not itself proof that the opposite fact exists. Record the analysis through the
existing evidence map and conclusions table; do not create an evidence-
evaluation register.

## A08 — Cases are supplemental and must be used substantively

The foundation is:

`matter facts + evidence + contract (if applicable) + law + legal subsumption = advocacy conclusion`

Cases supplement, explain or strengthen that foundation; case count, citation
ratio and the presence of a case are never a completion or quality-gate
condition. For a material, difficult or divided issue, conduct targeted case
research by default; otherwise research as needed. If research is restricted,
no usable case is found, or an opponent's case cannot be verified, record that
condition honestly and continue with supported facts, evidence and law.

Reuse `cn-case-hub`, `case-research-handoff` and current-law verification. If the
opponent supplied a case, check its identity/authenticity, actual adjudicative
reasons and applicability to this matter, then decide whether a response is
needed. Judge every case by the concrete adjudicative proposition, not merely
its overall result. An otherwise adverse case may still supply a reason
supporting an independent issue. A citation must identify the verified
court/adjudicative reason, key facts and the material correspondence to this
matter. Do not force an analogy across a material factual or legal difference,
pile up case numbers, or present an advocate's submission as the court's
holding. Maintain official-source, original-text, procedure-chain and
verification requirements. Research is two-sided internally; external citation
is selective and necessary, with high-relevance adverse cases retained in the
internal analysis.

## A09 — Procedure and stage variants

First apply R1 to identify procedure, represented role, stage, document variant
and task scope. Adapt the advocacy reasoning without copying general legal
rules already held elsewhere:

- pre-hearing: use current materials and do not invent a hearing, examination,
  evidentiary result or adjudicator view;
- post-hearing: use the actual hearing record, evidence comments, new opposing
  positions and issues the adjudicator actually raised. For a represented
  defendant/respondent with an already filed answer, default to the
  `post-hearing-advocacy-brief` two-source supplement described in A01: answer
  baseline, material actual oral assertions and late-filed written opinions;
  verify each source and record its locator. Organize by issue and, when a
  claim is duplicated, consolidate it while retaining both opposing-source
  locators (for example, the hearing-record page and the late-opinion
  page/section); link the answer paragraph separately when it is relevant.
  Briefly carry forward the existing response, then develop the supporting
  evidence and legal consequence. An explicit whole-case scope overrides that
  default; an explicit narrower scope uses `supplemental-advocacy-opinion`.
  Other post-hearing situations preserve the ordinary complete-versus-focused
  rule in the Skill entry. In every path, do not change confirmed facts,
  amounts or strategy, do not invent oral or written material, do not use the
  brief to replace a required evidence procedure, and record missing or
  unverified material and its effect honestly. Apply R2's sequential section
  confirmations, R8's deliverable and catalogue controls, and the existing
  A-Q/ART gate.
- civil first instance: establish the request or defence, elements, facts,
  evidence and legal consequence;
- civil appeal: an appellant identifies the original error, its basis, effect
  on the outcome and correction sought; a respondent explains why the appeal
  fails and cannot change the relevant result, without circularly relying only
  on the original conclusion;
- labour/personnel arbitration: address each request, labour-management facts,
  special burden rules and reproducible amount calculations;
- commercial arbitration: address the arbitration agreement/rules, contract,
  transaction context, performance and applicable law; do not transplant a
  court's internal case-retrieval duty; and
- retrial, enforcement objection and other special procedures: stay within the
  actual statutory review scope and remedy, rather than importing an ordinary
  first-instance structure.

## A10 — Judicial-document expression and useful density

Use conclusion-led headings that state the fact or legal conclusion the
decision-maker should adopt. Write in objective, precise language with legal
evaluation. Avoid emotional attacks, motive speculation, decorative modifiers,
unsupported absolutes and a neutral research-report voice. Give core disputes
the necessary facts, evidence, subsumption and response; summarize secondary
matters. Do not repeat the same facts, provision or reasoning in multiple
sections merely to add length. Use short cross-references where the full
analysis already appears. Judicial-document style does not impersonate the
court or turn the advocacy submission into a judgment.

## A11 — Reuse existing work and require sequential confirmation

Apply R2 and the existing workspace contract in this order:

`substantive analysis and necessary strategy confirmation → whole-document structure confirmation → draft and separately confirm each section → assemble only after all sections are confirmed → unified quality gate`

Section-by-section confirmation is mandatory. After a formal section is drafted,
obtain the user's explicit approval of that section before drafting the next
formal section. A known matter, completed hearing, confirmed litigation
position, confirmed outline or general drafting permission does not waive it.
R2's explicit draft-first exception does not waive structure or section
confirmation. Reuse the existing conclusions table, evidence map, legal/case
research, strategy and confirmation records. During final assembly, allow only
non-substantive changes to confirmed sections (such as numbering, transitions
and equivalent wording). If facts, core law, reasoning or conclusion changes,
reconfirm the affected section; unaffected sections do not need repeated
approval. Do not add an advocacy approval ledger, duplicate workflow or
parallel confirmation gate. Do not assemble or deliver the full text before
every section is confirmed.

## A12 — Integrated advocacy checks and ART

Do not create a separate advocacy quality gate. Apply the actual A-Q1–A-Q4 and
Adjudicative Reasoning Test (ART) criteria in the existing
[`pleading-quality-gate.md`](pleading-quality-gate.md) review and record the
result there with the other fact, evidence, law, amount, procedure, format,
template and release checks. A focused supplemental opinion is complete when it
resolves its declared issue; a complete brief must cover its full confirmed
scope. A static keyword match or checklist does not establish reliable
adjudicative reasoning. Any material unresolved issue holds the affected
section or final assembly under the existing gate, and passing A-Q1–A-Q4 never
waives existing controls or authorization.
